Similarly to the rule for Superiority Points in the Core Rulebook, this option introduces a stagger system into combat. Use it as a general rule change for the whole campaign, or as a gimmick for a specific boss.

With this rule, Vulnerabilities do not immediately increase Hit Point loss against more powerful foes: instead they can be exploited to pressure them toward a breaking point, where they become staggered — Vulnerable to most damage types, and losing their actions for the rest of the round. It does not apply to soldiers, which still function as normal; tracking pressure for soldiers is too fiddly.

Every elite and champion enters the scene with an empty Pressure Clock and a number of pressure points — weapon Categories especially useful against them (arcane, bow, brawling, dagger, firearm, flail, heavy, spear, sword, thrown).

- An elite has a 4-section Pressure Clock and two pressure points.
- A champion has a Pressure Clock of (2 + twice the number of soldiers they replace) sections, and four pressure points.

A massive military mech (champion 3) with a missile turret (soldier) and an energy shield projector (soldier). The turret and projector follow the normal rules and are both Vulnerable to fire. The mech is Vulnerable to bolt, has brawling, firearm, heavy and spear as pressure points, and a Pressure Clock of 8 sections.

Do not combine this with Category-based Affinities. The rule already interacts with weapon Categories.

- When a non-staggered enemy loses Hit Points to a type they are Vulnerable to, they do not lose double Hit Points. Instead their Pressure Clock fills by 1 section — or by 2 if the loss is equal to or higher than (10 + half that enemy's level).
- When a non-staggered enemy loses Hit Points to an attack with a weapon whose Categories match one or more of their pressure points, their Clock fills by 1 section.
- When a non-staggered enemy loses Hit Points to an effect involving one or more of their Banes, their Clock fills by 1 section.

These effects are not cumulative — if an attack qualifies for both, apply only the first, ignoring pressure points.

A level 25 enemy Vulnerable to ice, with daggers among its pressure points, is dealt 30 ice damage by a dagger. The threshold at that level is 22, so 2 sections fill. The pressure point is irrelevant here.

A Pressure Clock can also be filled by any method that fills sections of a Clock: the Objective action, the Progress opportunity, and Skills such as the Orator's Persuasive.

A Pressure Clock empties only at the end of the round in which the enemy has been staggered, or through custom effects that explicitly mention Pressure Clocks.

Keep such effects uncommon and hard to trigger. An enemy that erases 1 section at the end of each turn is extremely annoying; one that erases 1 section when it hits two or more targets with a once-per-round attack is fairer, if still challenging.

When an enemy's Pressure Clock becomes full, that enemy becomes staggered and suffers the following:

- They cannot take turns. Any remaining turns this round are wasted. If they become staggered during their own turn, that turn ends immediately.
- They treat neutral (−) and Resistance (RS) Affinities as Vulnerabilities, and all Vulnerabilities — preexisting and gained — cause double Hit Point loss as normal.
- Effects based on their turns do not end, begin or recharge. But a Guard is broken and its benefits end immediately, and a flying enemy is forced to land (it may resume flight at the end of the round).

At the end of each round, every staggered enemy erases all sections of their Pressure Clock and stops being staggered. It can be wise to wait and stagger a foe at the start of a new round to get the most out of it.

Where staggering meets an enemy's special rules, the Game Master has the final say — they chose or designed that enemy. Consider the likely interactions before the session.

Aside from trial and error, pressure points are revealed the same way as Affinities: any option that reveals a creature's Affinities — an Open Check, the Loremaster's Quick Assessment — works, as does any option that lets a player ask the Game Master a question and get a truthful answer.
